The ring ladder, as one strip

Two halves. Discover is the front page, the same signed in or out. The right half is your material, and its label is always the rung you are on — so the strip states what you are looking at instead of needing a separate dial. Click the right half to open the ladder.

Both frames are the same state at once, so the phone answer and the desktop answer can be judged against each other rather than one at a time. Signed out there is deliberately no strip — a guest has no graph to slice, and one half is not a strip. The masthead is drawn at its real 61px — the height it came down to when “Home” was removed, measured at 320px.

Why the label is the control

A separate tab plus a separate dial needs two glances and can disagree with itself. Folding them together means the right half always says exactly what it is showing you, and picking a rung both switches you there and sets the scope in one gesture.

Why it is not in the top bar

Up there it would govern one surface out of eleven and sit inert on the rest — a control that has to apologise on ten pages. Attached to the half it acts on, it disappears on Discover, where it would mean nothing.

What World means here

Not the firehose. Unsqueezed — everything in the material you have assembled: your posts, your follows, feeds you follow, hashtags you subscribe to. The composition is the boundary; the ladder narrows inside it.

The ladder, as sets

Each rung contains every rung above it. That ordering is the reason the current four rings had to be redefined: Mutuals +1 does not contain Following — someone you follow who follows nobody back, and who none of your mutuals follow, falls out — so stepping “further out” would have shown you less.
